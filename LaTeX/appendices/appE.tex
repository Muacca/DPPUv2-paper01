\section{Anisotropic torus and variation paths}\label{appE}
\subsection{General reduced function}
All spatial angles have period $2\pi$, and the circle coordinate has
period one. For $R_i>0$, define the axial torsion as $2\eta/R_1$.
This singles out $R_1$, so simply permuting the $R_i$ need not give
the same variation problem:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U_{\mathrm{FULL}}(R_1,R_2,R_3)
 &=Q R_2R_3\left(V^2R_1+\frac{9\eta^2}{R_1}\right),\\
U_{\mathrm{TT}}=U_{\mathrm{REE}}
 &=Q R_2R_3\left(V^2R_1+\frac{9\eta^2}{R_1}
                 +12V\eta\kappa^2\theta\right),\\
Q&=\frac{8\pi^3L}{3\kappa^2}>0
\end{aligned}
\tag{E-01}\label{eq:E-01}
\end{equation}
The quantities $\eta,V,L,\kappa,\theta$ are held fixed.

\subsection{Fixed shape and isotropic expansion}
For fixed $\alpha,\beta>0$, with $R_2=\alpha R_1$, $R_3=\beta R_1$,
the isotropic potential in Section~\ref{sec3} is multiplied by
$\alpha\beta$. Radius-extremum positions and types are unchanged,
while action values and heights acquire that factor.
There is no strict minimum in FULL along this path.

This equivalence concerns expansion with shape held fixed; it does not
imply stability in the $\alpha,\beta$ directions.
Other constraints, such as volume-preserving shape variations, require
their own reduced functions.

\subsection{Varying one radius with two radii fixed}
If $R_2,R_3$ are fixed and only $R_1$ varies, $V\eta\ne0$ gives
a strict minimum:
\begin{equation}
R_{1,0}=3\left\lvert\frac{\eta}{V}\right\rvert,\qquad
\frac{\partial^2U}{\partial R_1^2}
=Q R_2R_3\frac{18\eta^2}{R_1^3}>0.
\tag{E-02}\label{eq:E-02}
\end{equation}
The mixed term of the diagnostic actions is constant on this path,
so the minimum position and second derivative agree with FULL.
However, for $\eta\ne0$, $U\to+\infty$ as $R_1\to0$.
The Type~II cubic depth formula, which uses a zero origin limit,
does not apply to this minimum.

For $\eta=0,V\ne0$ the potential increases; for $V=0,\eta\ne0$
it decreases from positive values toward zero; when both vanish
it is flat. These cases are distinguished from isotropic expansion.

\subsection{Independent variations of all three radii}
Write $U=R_2R_3f(R_1)$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\partial_{R_1}U&=R_2R_3 f',&
\partial_{R_2}U&=R_3 f,&
\partial_{R_3}U&=R_2 f,\\
H_{22}&=H_{33}=0,&H_{23}&=f.
\end{aligned}
\tag{E-03}\label{eq:E-03}
\end{equation}
A minimum in $r$ or $R_1$ alone does not imply that the full gradient
vanishes. Since $H_{22}=H_{33}=0$, the Hessian in the three independent
variables cannot be positive definite.
In FULL, $f>0$ at the $R_1$ minimum with $V\eta\ne0$, so the other
two directions are not stationary.

Even if a diagnostic action satisfies the special conditions $f=f'=0$,
flat directions in $R_2,R_3$ remain. Such a point is not a strict
minimum in all variables.
With additional physical constraints on shape variations, stability
must be defined separately on the allowed subspace.
