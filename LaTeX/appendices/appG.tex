\section{Curvature in a noncoordinate frame}\label{appG}
\subsection{The structure-coefficient term}
The exterior derivative of $\omega^a{}_b=\Gamma^a{}_{bc}e^c$
contains both coefficient derivatives and a term from $de^c$:
\begin{equation}
d\omega^a{}_b
=d\Gamma^a{}_{bc}\wedge e^c
+\frac12\Gamma^a{}_{be}C^e{}_{cd}e^c\wedge e^d
\tag{G-01}\label{eq:G-01}
\end{equation}
Adding $\omega^a{}_e\wedge\omega^e{}_b$ and reading off two-form
components gives the term $+\Gamma^a{}_{be}C^e{}_{cd}$ in
\eqref{eq:M2-04}.
Constant frame components do not remove this term in a noncoordinate frame.
It vanishes for the torus, where $C=0$, but contributes differently on
$S^3$ and Nil, where $C\ne0$.

\subsection{Independence of signs, coefficients, and integration}
The structure-coefficient term in curvature, the torsion-input dictionary,
NY two-form coefficients, and global volume are defined separately.
Metric compatibility alone does not determine the NY contraction
coefficient, and agreement of local scalar curvatures does not
determine the integration volume.

Likewise, $T=-\tau$ preserves the connection; it does not make FULL
and REE the same action.
The actions that coincide on the ansatz are the TT and positive-REE
diagnostics. The FULL NY density vanishes as their density difference.
