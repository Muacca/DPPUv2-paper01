\section{Introduction}\label{sec1}
\subsection{Question and scope}
We study reduced potentials in Einstein--Cartan (EC) gravity with a constant
Nieh--Yan (NY) coupling on static, homogeneous Euclidean geometries
$S^3\times S^1$, $T^3\times S^1$, and compact $\mathrm{Nil}^3\times S^1$.
The connection is metric compatible and admits axial and vector torsion.
The question is how the extrema of a specified one-parameter restriction of
the action depend on the geometry, the torsion parameters, and the chosen
action variant.

Throughout this paper, a \emph{phase} means an extremum class on the open
positive-radius domain. It denotes neither a thermodynamic phase,
a transition between topologies, nor a classification of solutions
of the full field equations. We define $U=-S$
after integration over all four dimensions. Without a kinetic term, a time
prescription, constraints, and a physical unit dictionary, this quantity
cannot be identified with an energy or a particle mass.

\subsection{Torsion and comparison of geometries}
The same torsion ansatz is used on the three spatial geometries.
Local structure coefficients and curvature are specified separately from
the global quotient, coordinate periods, and integration volume.
The EC framework and the NY identity provide the underlying geometric
setting \cite{Hehl1976,NiehYan1982,Chandia1997}.

In the preceding version of this paper and its computational implementation,
the input array was called $T$. With the Cartan equation and index order
used here, the torsion reconstructed from that connection is the negative
of this array. We denote the input array by $\tau$ and use $T$ for geometric
torsion, so that $\tau=-T$. The parameters $V,\eta$ in the main text describe
$T$. Appendix~\ref{appA} gives the connection dictionary, and
Appendix~\ref{appC} gives the input-domain dictionary.

This dictionary preserves the formulas used to construct the contortion
and connection from the code input: simultaneous conversion of the array
and its parameters describes the same connection. It is distinct from
the correction of the NY contraction coefficient, which changes the
reduced potentials.

\subsection{Principal conclusions}
On the ansatz considered here, the TT and positive-REE densities coincide
pointwise. Their difference, the complete NY density, vanishes.
Consequently, the complete action, denoted FULL, has no $\theta$-dependent
phase boundary. For fixed torsion parameters and circle length,
$S^3$ can support a strict radius minimum through its negative linear
term and the positive cubic vector-torsion term. The isotropic or
fixed-shape $T^3$ path and the uniform Nil path do not support such a
minimum in FULL.

The separate TT and positive-REE actions are diagnostic comparisons.
Their mixed term is proportional to $V\eta\theta$, and they can have
barriers. Those barriers are not properties of FULL. The comparison is
geometry dependent: the global quotient and the path of variation are
part of the problem. It does not establish vacuum selection by topology.

\subsection{Main points of this revision}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The coefficient in the REE contraction is $1/2$, replacing $1/4$;
the TT coefficient remains $1/4$. For this ansatz the complete NY density
therefore vanishes.
\item Geometric torsion $T$ is the primary variable. The relation
$\tau=-T$ preserves the connection associated with the computational
input and is independent of the NY coefficient correction.
\item Global normalization is fixed explicitly: the round-sphere radius
is $a=r/2$, $L$ is the circle circumference,
$\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\times S^1)=\pi^2Lr^3/4$, and the spatial torus
angles have period $2\pi$, giving volume $(2\pi)^3LR_1R_2R_3$.
For Nil, the lattice $(a,b,c)=(2\pi,2\pi,4\pi^2)$ realizes the volume
$(2\pi)^4LR^3$.
\item Claims of $\theta$-dependent stabilization and phase boundaries in
FULL are withdrawn. Diagnostic actions are distinguished from FULL,
and radius minima are distinguished from full vacuum solutions.
Potential heights are not directly interpreted as masses or decay rates.
\end{enumerate}
