\section{Conclusions and open questions}\label{sec8}
For the static homogeneous axial-plus-vector ansatz on
$S^3\times S^1$, $T^3\times S^1$, and compact $\mathrm{Nil}^3\times S^1$,
the TT and positive-REE densities coincide pointwise.
The complete NY density vanishes, so the FULL reduced potential is
independent of its constant coupling $\theta$.

At fixed $\eta,V,L,\kappa,\theta$, FULL has a strict sphere radius
minimum precisely when $V\ne0$ and $|\eta|<2$; this is Type~II.
It has no strict minimum along the isotropic or fixed-shape torus
path or the uniform Nil path.
The torus path with two radii fixed is a different restriction and can
have a radius minimum. None of these radius statements establishes
stability under all variations.

The diagnostic TT and positive-REE potentials coincide and can support
Type~I barriers under the conditions in Section~\ref{sec4}.
The cubic analysis distinguishes strict minima, boundary roots,
stationary inflections, and degenerate linear cases without relying
on a finite scan.
These diagnostic barriers must not be attributed to the complete NY action.

The normalization is part of each geometric definition:
$a=r/2$ for the sphere, spatial periods $2\pi$ for the torus,
the Nil lattice $(2\pi,2\pi,4\pi^2)$, and the actual circle circumference $L$.
The geometric torsion $T$ and code input $\tau=-T$ describe the same
connection after simultaneous parameter conversion.

The results provide a method for comparing geometric reductions.
They do not identify a full vacuum, a particle, or a topology selected
by physical dynamics.
Under the restricted source-free, constant-NY, real Euclidean assumptions
of Section~\ref{sec7}, a smooth nondegenerate full vacuum on
$S^3\times S^1$ is excluded; the off-shell reductions remain well defined.

Open physical questions include the definition of energy, mass, and
decay observables, and the common constraints needed to compare
different topology sectors. These require additional dynamical and
physical input beyond the radius potentials established here.
