\section{Geometric Interpretation and Utility as a Tool}
\label{sec:interpretation}

This section interprets the numerical results obtained in Secs.~\ref{sec:numerical}--\ref{sec:representative} 
from a geometric perspective and discusses the applicability and limitations 
of the diagnostic framework provided by this work.

\subsection{Geometric Factors of Topology Dependence}
\label{sec:geometric_factors}

complexity of phase structure.

\subsubsection{Role of background curvature}

The background Ricci scalar $R_{\LC}$ from the Levi--Civita connection affects
the effective potential through the Einstein--Hilbert term $R/(2\kappa^2)$,
which determines the geometric coefficient $C$ in each topology.
(Note that $N_{\REE} = -2V\eta/r$ is topology-independent after the
torsion reduction, so background curvature enters $\Veff$ solely via
the Ricci scalar.)

\paragraph{Positive curvature ($\Sthree$): Lowers the barrier}

For $\Sthree$ ($R_{\LC} = +24/r^2$), the positive curvature contributes a negative constant $-36$
to the geometric coefficient $C = 9\eta^2 - 36$.
This "lowering" of the barrier allows $C$ to become negative for small $\eta$,
creating a potential well even without torsion. This is the origin of the
robust stability of spherical space.

\paragraph{Zero curvature ($\Tthree$): Neutral baseline}

For $\Tthree$ ($R_{\LC} = 0$), $C = 9\eta^2 \ge 0$.
The baseline is neutral. Stability is possible but requires the torsion term $B$
to actively dig the well, necessitating $\theta_{\NY} > \theta_{crit}$.

\paragraph{Negative curvature ($\Nilthree$): Raises the barrier}

For $\Nilthree$ ($R_{\LC} = -1/2r^2$), the negative curvature contributes a positive constant $+3$
to $C = 36\eta^2 + 3$.
This "raising" of the barrier makes stabilization harder, specifically forbidding it
near $\eta=0$ where the torsion term is weak. This "negative curvature-related suppression"
is a distinct geometric fingerprint.

\subsubsection{Isotropy and anisotropy}

$\Sthree$ (SU(2)) is an isotropic homogeneous space where any two directions
can be mapped to each other by group action. On the other hand, $\Nilthree$
(Heisenberg) is anisotropic, with the structure $[E_0, E_1] = (1/R)E_2$
giving special treatment to the ``$E_2$ direction.''

The anisotropy manifests in the effective potential as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Sthree$: $C(\eta) = 9\eta^2 - 36$ (allows $C < 0$ for $|\eta| < 2$)
  \item $\Nilthree$: $C(\eta) = 36\eta^2 + 3$ (always positive, larger coefficient)
\end{itemize}

For anisotropic topologies like $\Nilthree$, stable minima require larger
$\theta_{\NY}$ values to overcome the always-positive $C$ term.

\subsection{Role of FULL Revealed by Diagnostic Comparison (TT/REE)}
\label{sec:FULL_role}

\subsubsection{Physical meaning of TT and REE}

In the decomposition $N = N_{\TT} - N_{\REE}$ of the NY density, each component 
carries different geometric information:

\paragraph{$N_{\TT}$ (torsion-torsion term):}
\begin{equation}
  N_{\TT} = \frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{abcd} T^{e}{}_{ab} T_{ecd}
\end{equation}

This represents ``self-interaction'' of torsion and does not depend on 
background curvature. It takes the form $N_{\TT} \propto V\eta/r$ and is 
an odd function of $\eta$.

\paragraph{$N_{\REE}$ (Riemann-torsion term):}
\begin{equation}
  N_{\REE} = \frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{abcd} R_{abcd}
\end{equation}

This is the contribution of curvature from the EC connection (including torsion).
All three topologies have the same simplified form:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Sthree$: $N_{\REE} \propto V\eta/r$
  \item $\Tthree$: $N_{\REE} \propto V\eta/r$
  \item $\Nilthree$: $N_{\REE} \propto V\eta/r$
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Unified form for $B$ }

The three variants (FULL, TT, REE) have similar $B$ forms. For all topologies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item FULL: $B \propto \eta$
  \item TT: $B \propto 2\eta$
  \item REE: $B \propto \eta$
\end{itemize}

All variants share the same sign condition: $B < 0$ for $\eta < 0$,
when $\theta_{\NY} > 0$.

The exact derivative structure $N = \dd(e^a \wedgep T_a)$ of the NY term
still carries geometric information, but the topology-dependent differences
now appear primarily in the coefficient $C$ rather than in the form of $B$.

\subsubsection{Structural origin of $N_{\FULL} = N_{\REE}$}

All three topologies have $N_{\FULL} = N_{\REE} = -2V\eta/r$.
This identity is not a numerical coincidence but a structural consequence
of the $\mathcal{M}_3 \times \Sone$ product geometry and the NY Lagrangian.

Since $N_{\FULL} = N_{\TT} - N_{\REE}$ by definition, the identity
$N_{\FULL} = N_{\REE}$ is equivalent to $N_{\TT} = 2 N_{\REE}$.
To see why this holds for all topologies, consider the decomposition of
$N_{\REE}$:
\begin{equation}
  N_{\REE} = \frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{abcd} R^{\mathrm{EC}}_{abcd}
  = \frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{abcd} R^{\LC}_{abcd}
  + (\text{contortion-dependent terms}).
\end{equation}
In the $\mathcal{M}_3 \times \Sone$ product geometry, the Levi--Civita
Riemann tensor $R^{\LC}_{abcd}$ is supported entirely on the spatial
$\mathcal{M}_3$ directions (indices $0,1,2$), with all components
involving the $\Sone$ direction (index $3$) vanishing. The totally
antisymmetric tensor $\varepsilon^{abcd}$ in four dimensions, however,
requires all four distinct indices, forcing one to be the $\Sone$ direction.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon^{abcd} R^{\LC}_{abcd} = 0
  \label{eq:LC_pontryagin_vanish}
\end{equation}
identically for all topologies. This eliminates the topology-dependent
contribution to $N_{\REE}$, leaving only contortion (torsion) terms that
depend on $(V, \eta)$ but not on the spatial geometry. Since $N_{\TT}$
is also purely torsional by construction, both components---and hence
$N_{\FULL}$---are topology-independent.

This result has an important physical implication: the stabilization
driven by the NY term is a property of the torsion sector alone, with
topology entering the effective potential exclusively through the
Einstein--Hilbert (geometric) coefficient $C$. The phase diagrams
(Figs.~\ref{fig:phase_matrix_04to05}--\ref{fig:phase_matrix_08to09})
confirm this separation: all three NY variants produce qualitatively
similar phase boundaries, differing only in the numerical prefactor
of $B$ ($B_{\TT} = 2 B_{\FULL}$).

\subsection{Usage as a Reusable Diagnostic Tool}
\label{sec:diagnostic_tool}

\subsubsection{Input and output specifications}

The diagnostic framework provided by this work has the following input/output 
specifications:

\paragraph{Input:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Topology: $\mathcal{M}_3 \in \{\Sthree, \Tthree, \Nilthree\}$ 
        (or specified by structure constants $C^{i}{}_{jk}$)
  \item Parameters: $(V, \eta, \theta_{\NY})$
  \item NY variant: FULL / TT / REE
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Output:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Type classification: I (stable with barrier) / II (rolling) / 
        III (unstable)
  \item Stable radius: $r_0$ (for Type I/II)
  \item Barrier height: $\Delta V$ (for Type I)
  \item Effective potential: Analytical expression for $\Veff(r)$
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Application example: Use for parameter design}

When constructing cosmological models based on EC+NY theory, this diagnostic 
framework can be used for the following purposes:

\paragraph{Identification of stable regions:}
For a given topology and NY variant, read the parameter range realizing 
Type I or Type II from phase diagrams.

\paragraph{Prediction of critical conditions:}
Use the analytical boundary conditions derived in Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanisms} 
(e.g., $\eta = 2, -2$ for $\Sthree$) to predict parameters where Type 
transitions occur.

\paragraph{Evaluation of stability quality:}
Quantitatively evaluate the ``robustness'' of metastable states from 
$\log_{10}(\Delta V)$ values. Larger $\Delta V$ means stronger suppression 
of decay by quantum tunneling.

\subsubsection{Extension to other topologies}

This diagnostic framework can be extended to any 3-dimensional Lie group 
admitting left-invariant coframes. Specifically:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Specify structure constants $C^{i}{}_{jk}$
  \item Execute the computational pipeline from Sec.~\ref{sec:pipeline}
  \item Derive effective potential $\Veff(r)$
  \item Compute Type classification and stability metrics
\end{enumerate}

Among Thurston's eight geometries, those with compact quotients 
($\Sthree$, $\mathbb{E}^3$, $\mathrm{Nil}$, $\mathrm{Sol}$, 
$\widetilde{SL_2(\mathbb{R})}$, $\mathbb{H}^2 \times \mathbb{R}$, 
$S^2 \times \mathbb{R}$) can in principle be treated with this framework. 
However, $S^2 \times \mathbb{R}$ is not parallelizable, requiring caution 
in constructing a global coframe.
