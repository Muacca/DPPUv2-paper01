\section{Variation scope and physical interpretation}\label{sec7}
\subsection{A radius minimum is not full stationarity}
On $S^3$ in FULL, take $V=1$, $\eta=\theta=0$, and $\kappa=L=1$.
At $r=2\sqrt3$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\partial_r U&=0,&\partial_r^2U&=\sqrt3\,\pi^2>0,\\
\partial_V U&=4\sqrt3\,\pi^2\ne0,
&\partial_L U&=-4\sqrt3\,\pi^2\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M7-01}\label{eq:M7-01}
\end{equation}
Thus radius stationarity and positive radial curvature coexist with
nonzero derivatives in other directions.
On $T^3$, fixed-shape scaling and variation of the three radii independently
are different questions. In particular, Appendix~\ref{appE} shows that
the full radius Hessian has $H_{22}=H_{33}=0$ on the specified family.

\subsection{Scoped nonexistence of a full vacuum on the sphere product}
The following statement concerns standard EC gravity with a constant
complete NY coupling, no sources, and no cosmological constant,
on a closed real Euclidean $S^3\times S^1$.
The coframe is smooth and nondegenerate and the metric-compatible
connection is varied independently.

The complete NY term is a total derivative, so a constant coefficient
does not change the bulk equations on a closed manifold.
For a nondegenerate coframe, the standard EC connection equation gives
zero torsion, and the coframe equation gives Ricci flatness.
For these bulk variations and the NY identity, see
\cite[Sec.~IV.A--B, Eqs.~(4.10)--(4.12), (4.20)]{Corichi2016}.
We now apply their geometric consequences under the real Euclidean,
closed-manifold assumptions stated above.

Since $\chi(S^3\times S^1)=0$, the four-dimensional Gauss--Bonnet
formula for a Ricci-flat Riemannian metric gives zero for a positive
constant times the integral of the squared Riemann-curvature norm.
The nonnegative integrand must therefore vanish everywhere, so the
metric would be flat.

By Bieberbach's theorem, a compact flat four-manifold has a finite
cover by a flat $T^4$; see also the proof of Lemma~1 in \cite{LeBrun2009}.
Its fundamental group must contain a finite-index subgroup
isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^4$.
But $\pi_1(S^3\times S^1)=\mathbb Z$, whose subgroups are cyclic and
cannot be isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^4$.
This contradiction excludes a full vacuum satisfying all the stated
assumptions. It is a global obstruction, not an inference from a finite
set of parameter examples.
It does not exclude the off-shell geometries used in this paper,
and does not address theories with a cosmological constant, sources,
boundaries, complex metrics, nonconstant $\theta$, or higher-curvature terms.

\subsection{NY, curvature contractions, and topology}
The contraction $\varepsilon^{abcd}R_{abcd}$ is linear in curvature.
It is not the Pontryagin density, which is quadratic in curvature
through $R\wedge R$.
Its vanishing on a torsion-free slice does not imply vanishing for
general torsion.
Likewise, a zero integral of the complete NY form does not require
the separate TT and REE integrals to vanish.

\subsection{What is required for physical observables}
The sign of $U_{\min}$ alone does not identify a dominant saddle in a
path integral. Such an interpretation requires an integration contour,
a weighting prescription, and the relevant variations.
Nor does a height difference $\Delta U$ give a physical energy or
a tunneling rate without a kinetic term, constraints, boundary conditions,
and a bounce or an equivalent dynamical analysis.
The Type~I action difference alone does not determine a WKB exponent.
Fixing the global volume does not supply that physical unit dictionary.

Comparing topology sectors requires a common physical framework,
including the quantities and constraints held fixed.
An extension to $S^2\times\mathbb R$ would also require a noncompact-volume
prescription and a defined reduction.
Nontriviality of $TS^2$ alone does not prove the absence of a global frame
on a product containing it. No such extension is computed here.
