\section{Representative Points and Stability Quality}
\label{sec:representative}

This section presents the specific shapes of the effective potential $\Veff(r)$ 
and stability metrics at representative parameter points on the phase diagrams. 
This clarifies how the operational definitions of Type I/II/III 
(Sec.~\ref{sec:type_definition}) correspond to actual potential shapes in the 
light of the corrected stability mechanism dominated by torsion.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Selection Criteria for Representative Points}
\label{sec:selection_criteria}

For each topology, we select representative points to illustrate the typical 
behaviors of stable and unstable vacua. We set the coupling constant $V = 4.0$ 
and fix the compactification scale $L=1$ (and $\kappa=1$).
The selection criteria are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Type I representative}: Points exhibiting a metastable well with 
        a distinct potential barrier ($\Delta V > 0$). We focus on the region 
        $\eta < 0$ where the torsion-induced stability mechanism is most effective.
  \item \textbf{Type II representative}: Points where the potential minimum exists 
        but the barrier disappears or becomes negligible, typically found near 
        stability boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Type III representative}: Points showing instability where no 
        minimum forms within the allowed dynamical range.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Note on S2:} This point lies exactly on the Type I/II boundary where the
coefficient $C = 9\eta^2 - 36$ vanishes. At $C = 0$, the effective potential
satisfies $\dd\Veff/\dd r|_{r \to 0^+} = 0$ but $\dd^2\Veff/\dd r^2|_{r=0} < 0$,
so the potential is flat at the origin and immediately decreasing---a degenerate
case between barrier (Type I) and rolling (Type II).

\subsubsection{Characteristics of each representative point}

\paragraph{Point S1 (Type I, $\eta = -3$):}
This point represents a robust stable vacuum. The negative flux parameter
$\eta = -3$ generates a large negative term in the potential via the torsion
contribution ($B \propto \eta$), creating a deep potential well protected by a
high barrier. This confirms that $\Sthree$ can host stable vacua in the negative
$\eta$ region, driven by the flux-induced torsion.

\paragraph{Point S2 (I/II boundary, $\eta = -2$):}
At $\eta = -2$, the coefficient $C = 9\eta^2 - 36 = 0$ exactly, placing this
point on the Type I/II phase boundary. The minimum at $r_0 = 2.00$ persists,
but the barrier vanishes. This is the critical $\eta$ value at which the
positive background curvature of $\Sthree$ ceases to provide geometric
stabilization (for $|\eta| > 2$, $C > 0$ and a barrier forms; for $|\eta| < 2$,
$C < 0$ and the potential rolls from the origin).

\paragraph{Point S3 (Type II, $\eta = 0$):}
As $\eta$ increases toward zero, the stabilizing torsion
contribution weakens. At $\eta = 0$, only the structure constant
term contributes to the quadratic coefficient ($C = -36 < 0$), forming a minimum but
lacking the barrier characteristic of the torsion-dominated region.

\paragraph{Point S4 (Type III, $\eta = 3$):}
For positive $\eta$, both the curvature term $C$ and the torsion term $B$ 
contribute positively, leading to a monotonic potential without a minimum. 
This illustrates the general instability of the $\eta > 0$ region for $\Sthree$.

This point is located near the stability boundary. While it remains Type I, 
the barrier height ($\Delta V \approx 19$) is orders of magnitude smaller than 
at N1. This illustrates the transition towards instability as $\eta$ approaches 
the critical value where the negative torsion term can no longer overcome the 
positive geometric terms.

At $\eta = 0$, the negative torsion term vanishes. The inherent negative scalar 
curvature of $\Nilthree$ is insufficient to stabilize the modulus in this 
configuration without the aid of flux, resulting in Type III instability.

In all three topologies, we observe a strong positive correlation:
\begin{equation}
 \log \Delta V \sim \alpha \log r_0 + \text{const.}
\end{equation}
This indicates that deeper potentials (larger $\Delta V$) are associated with 
larger moduli radii ($r_0$). Physically, this corresponds to the regime where 
large negative $\eta$ drives the minimum to larger values while simultaneously 
deepening the potential well. The linearity in the log-log plot suggests a 
power-law behavior $\Delta V \propto r_0^\alpha$, which is consistent with the
polynomial structure of the effective potential
$\Veff(r) \propto r(Ar^2 + Br + C)$.

\subsection{Consistency Check Between Analytical Boundaries and Numerical Scan}
\label{sec:consistency_check}

We verified the consistency between the analytical conditions derived in 
Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanisms} and the numerical classification results.

\subsubsection{S3 Stability Boundary}
The analytical condition $C(\eta) = 0$ for $\Sthree$ (with $V=4$) yields 
$\eta = -2$ and $\eta = +2$ as critical points.
Numerical scans confirm that around $\eta = -2$, the potential behavior changes
significantly: S2 ($\eta=-2$) sits exactly on the I/II boundary where $C = 0$,
with $r_0 = 2.00$ matching the analytical prediction
$r_0 = -2B/(3A) = 96/48 = 2$.

\subsubsection{T3 Instability Threshold}
For $\Tthree$, the stability condition requires a sufficiently large flux parameter 
$\theta_{\NY}$ to overcome the flat geometry. Our numerical check at $\eta = -3$ 
confirms that stable Type I vacua appear only when $\theta_{\NY} \gtrsim 0.8$, 
which is fully consistent with the analytical threshold $\theta_{\NY} \approx 0.87$.

\subsubsection{Nil3 Stability Limit}
For $\Nilthree$, the competition between the positive curvature term ($C > 0$) 
and the negative torsion term ($B < 0$ for $\eta < 0$) determines stability.
The numerical scan shows that stability (Type I) is maintained down to 
$\eta \approx -0.6$ (Point N2), but is lost by $\eta = -0.5$. This aligns with 
the requirement that the torsion effect must be strong enough (sufficiently negative $\eta$) 
to create a local minimum.

\subsection{Summary of Stability Quality}
\label{sec:representative_summary}

The analysis of representative points and stability metrics reveals a consistent 
physical picture across $\Sthree$, $\Tthree$, and $\Nilthree$:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Universal Stability in $\eta < 0$}: All three topologies exhibit 
    robust Type I stable vacua in the region of large negative $\eta$. 
    This stability is driven by the flux-induced torsion term.
    \item \textbf{Topology Independence at Large $|\eta|$}: The similarity between 
    S1, T1, and N1 parameters suggests that strong flux effects can override 
    differences in spatial curvature.
    \item \textbf{Consistent Scaling}: The positive correlation between barrier 
    height and stable radius is a universal feature of these flux-stabilized vacua.
\end{enumerate}

These results provide solid numerical evidence for the torsion-dominated stability 
mechanism proposed in this paper.
