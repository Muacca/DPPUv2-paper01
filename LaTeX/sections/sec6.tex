\section{Representative parameters and scaling}\label{sec6}
\subsection{A FULL sphere minimum}
Set $\kappa=L=1$ and use geometric-torsion parameters throughout.
For $V=-4$, $\eta=0$, and arbitrary $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
r_0=\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\qquad
a_0=\frac{\sqrt3}{4},\qquad
U(r_0)=-\sqrt3\,\pi^2,\qquad
\Delta U_{\mathrm{II}}=\sqrt3\,\pi^2.
\tag{M6-01}\label{eq:M6-01}
\end{equation}
This is a Type~II minimum. Its positive depth is measured relative to
the algebraic origin as in Section~\ref{sec5}.
At $\eta=\pm2$, the FULL potential has only its positive cubic term.
At $\eta=\pm3$, its linear term is also positive, and there is no
strict positive-radius minimum. FULL does not select one sign of $\eta$.

\subsection{Torus and Nil examples}
For the isotropic torus with $V=-4$, $\eta=0$,
$\mathcal R_{\mathrm{EC}}=-32/3$ and
$U=128\pi^3r^3/3$. The vanishing Levi--Civita curvature does not imply
vanishing torsionful curvature or a flat potential.
The FULL potential on Nil is increasing for every real $\eta,V$.

For $V=-4$, $\eta=3$, and $\kappa^2\theta=1$, the diagnostic action
has Type~I behavior on all three geometries, while FULL has no strict
minimum on any of the three paths.
At $\eta=0$ or $V=0$, the mixed density vanishes and all variants coincide.
This does not imply equality of FULL and REE for general MX inputs.

\subsection{Scaling with vector torsion}
For $V\ne0$, define $x=|V|r$ and $\sigma=\operatorname{sign}V$.
The diagnostic sphere potential becomes
\begin{equation}
U^{S^3}_{\mathrm{TT}}
=\frac{\pi^2L}{12\kappa^2\lvert V\rvert}
\left[x^3+12\sigma\eta\kappa^2\theta x^2+(9\eta^2-36)x\right].
\tag{M6-02}\label{eq:M6-02}
\end{equation}
At fixed $\eta,\kappa^2\theta,\sigma$ (and fixed $\kappa,L$), extremum
radii and potential-height differences scale as $1/|V|$, provided the
corresponding minimum exists.
The same substitution applies to the isotropic torus and Nil potentials.
For example, there is no FULL minimum at $\eta=3$ to which a minimum
scaling law could be applied.
The case $V=0$ is a separate linear problem.
