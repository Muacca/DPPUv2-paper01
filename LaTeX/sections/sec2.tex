\section{Action, connection, and variations}\label{sec2}
\subsection{Coframe and index conventions}
We use the four-dimensional Euclidean metric $\delta_{ab}$, with
$a,b,c=0,1,2,3$ and spatial indices $i,j,k=0,1,2$.
For an orthonormal coframe $e^a$ and its dual frame $E_a$,
$\varepsilon_{0123}=+1$ and
$\mathrm{vol}_4=e^0\wedge e^1\wedge e^2\wedge e^3$.
The structure coefficients have the positive-sign convention
\begin{equation}
de^a=\frac12 C^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c,\qquad
[E_b,E_c]=-C^a{}_{bc}E_a,\qquad
\omega^a{}_b=\Gamma^a{}_{bc}e^c.
\tag{M2-01}\label{eq:M2-01}
\end{equation}
The last index of the connection coefficient is the differentiation
direction. Metric compatibility means $\Gamma_{abc}=-\Gamma_{bac}$.
The components of the torsion and curvature two-forms are defined by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T^a&=de^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b
     =\frac12 T^a{}_{bc}e^b\wedge e^c,\\
R^a{}_b&=d\omega^a{}_b+\omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b
     =\frac12 R^a{}_{bcd}e^c\wedge e^d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M2-02}\label{eq:M2-02}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Reconstructing the connection}
The connection constructed from geometric torsion is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma_{abc}&=\Gamma^{\mathrm{LC}}_{abc}+K_{abc}[T],\\
\Gamma^{\mathrm{LC}}_{abc}
 &=\frac12(C_{abc}+C_{cba}-C_{bac}),\\
K_{abc}[T]&=-\frac12(T_{abc}+T_{bca}-T_{cab}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{M2-03}\label{eq:M2-03}
\end{equation}
The sign follows from \eqref{eq:M2-01}, \eqref{eq:M2-02}, and the index
order. Substitution in
$T^a{}_{bc}=C^a{}_{bc}+\Gamma^a{}_{cb}-\Gamma^a{}_{bc}$
recovers the prescribed $T$. The curvature includes the noncoordinate term:
\begin{equation}
R^a{}_{bcd}
=E_c(\Gamma^a{}_{bd})-E_d(\Gamma^a{}_{bc})
+\Gamma^a{}_{ec}\Gamma^e{}_{bd}
-\Gamma^a{}_{ed}\Gamma^e{}_{bc}
+\Gamma^a{}_{be}C^e{}_{cd}.
\tag{M2-04}\label{eq:M2-04}
\end{equation}
Frame components are constant at each fixed scale in this ansatz, so
the first two derivative terms vanish. Comparing different geometries
by varying the scale is a different operation from differentiation
in a frame direction.

\subsection{Torsion ansatz}
We specify geometric torsion as the sum of axial and vector parts.
Here $s=r$ for $S^3$, $s=R_1$ for $T^3$, and $s=R$ for Nil.
The axial part has spatial indices only:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T^{\mathrm{ax}}_{ijk}&=\frac{2\eta}{s}\varepsilon_{ijk},
&T^{\mathrm{ax}}_{ab3}&=T^{\mathrm{ax}}_{a3b}=T^{\mathrm{ax}}_{3ab}=0,\\
T^{\mathrm{vec}}_{abc}&=\frac13(\delta_{ac}V_b-\delta_{ab}V_c),
&V_a&=(0,0,0,V),\\
T_{abc}&=T^{\mathrm{ax}}_{abc}+T^{\mathrm{vec}}_{abc}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M2-05}\label{eq:M2-05}
\end{equation}
The parameters $\eta,V$ are signed real numbers, and
$T^\lambda{}_{\mu\lambda}=V_\mu$.
AX denotes $V=0$, VT denotes $\eta=0$, and MX allows both parts.
Zero values are included in the formulas. Restricting $V$ to
nonnegative values is a choice of input domain, not a geometric definition.

We hold $\eta,V$ fixed in the reduced variation. Thus the axial frame
component $2\eta/s$ changes with scale. This differs both from holding
all torsion frame components fixed and from freely varying an independent
connection.

\subsection{Complete Nieh--Yan term and diagnostic actions}
The NY four-form and its component densities are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N&=d(e^a\wedge T_a)
=T^a\wedge T_a-e^a\wedge e^b\wedge R_{ab}
=n_{\mathrm{FULL}}\,\mathrm{vol}_4,\\
n_{\mathrm{TT}}&=\frac14\varepsilon^{abcd}T^e{}_{ab}T_{ecd},\\
n_{\mathrm{REE}}&=\frac12\varepsilon^{abcd}R_{abcd},\\
n_{\mathrm{FULL}}&=n_{\mathrm{TT}}-n_{\mathrm{REE}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M2-06}\label{eq:M2-06}
\end{equation}
The TT factor $1/4$ comes from the two torsion two-forms, while the REE
factor $1/2$ comes from the single curvature two-form.
Pointwise on the present ansatz,
\begin{equation}
n_{\mathrm{TT}}=n_{\mathrm{REE}}=-\frac{4V\eta}{s},
\qquad n_{\mathrm{FULL}}=0.
\tag{M2-07}\label{eq:M2-07}
\end{equation}
For $\kappa^2>0$ and a spacetime-constant $\theta$, the complete action is
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{FULL}}
=\int_M\frac{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{EC}}}{2\kappa^2}\,\mathrm{vol}_4
+\theta\int_M\mathcal N,\qquad U_{\mathrm{FULL}}=-S_{\mathrm{FULL}}.
\tag{M2-08}\label{eq:M2-08}
\end{equation}
For comparison we define
$S_{\mathrm{TT}}=\int_M(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{EC}}/(2\kappa^2)
+\theta n_{\mathrm{TT}})\mathrm{vol}_4$
and
$S_{\mathrm{REE}}=\int_M(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{EC}}/(2\kappa^2)
+\theta n_{\mathrm{REE}})\mathrm{vol}_4$, with $U=-S$ in each case.
The REE diagnostic adds the curvature-side density with a \emph{positive}
sign. It therefore differs in sign from isolating the negative component
of the complete NY term. With this definition,
$U_{\mathrm{TT}}=U_{\mathrm{REE}}$ on the ansatz.

If $e^a\wedge T_a$ is globally defined and smooth on a closed manifold,
Stokes' theorem makes the complete NY integral zero.
Here its density also vanishes pointwise. This conclusion is not transferred
unchanged to problems with boundaries, singularities, or a nonconstant coupling.

\subsection{Integration, dimensions, and variation domain}
The common circle coordinate is $u\in[0,1)$, with $e^3=L\,du$.
Thus $L>0$ is its actual circumference, and the action includes integration
over this direction. In length units, $\eta$ is dimensionless,
$V$ has dimension inverse length, and $\kappa^2$ has dimension length squared.
For the action in \eqref{eq:M2-08} to be dimensionless, $\theta$ has dimension
inverse length squared; $\kappa^2\theta$ is dimensionless.
Setting $\kappa=L=1$ in numerical examples selects reference units.
It does not identify $U$ with a Planck mass.

The main variation paths change $r$ on $S^3$, $R$ on Nil, and either
$R_i=r$ or $R_2=\alpha R_1$, $R_3=\beta R_1$ on $T^3$.
The positive shape ratios $\alpha,\beta$ and $\eta,V,L,\kappa,\theta$
are fixed. Appendix~\ref{appE} separately treats the torus path with
$R_2,R_3$ fixed.

Stationarity of the one-variable action after substitution does not replace
the Euler--Lagrange equations for the independent connection, all coframe
components, $L$, or the torsion coefficients. We call the resulting extrema
radius minima or reduced minima, distinguishing them from full vacuum solutions.
