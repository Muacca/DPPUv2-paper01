\section{Global geometry and reduced potentials}\label{sec3}
\subsection{The three-sphere: structure coefficients and physical radius}
Identify $S^3$ with $SU(2)$ and take Euler angles
$\vartheta\in[0,\pi]$, $\varphi\in[0,2\pi)$, $\psi\in[0,4\pi)$.
Use the left-invariant forms
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda^0&=\cos\psi\,d\vartheta+\sin\psi\sin\vartheta\,d\varphi,\\
\lambda^1&=-\sin\psi\,d\vartheta+\cos\psi\sin\vartheta\,d\varphi,\\
\lambda^2&=d\psi+\cos\vartheta\,d\varphi,\qquad
e^i=-\frac r4\lambda^i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M3-01}\label{eq:M3-01}
\end{equation}
At the boundaries, $(\varphi+2\pi,\psi)\sim(\varphi,\psi+2\pi)$,
together with the usual $SU(2)$ identifications at the polar coordinate
degeneracies. These angles are not three independent circles.
The coframe gives $C^i{}_{jk}=4\varepsilon_{ijk}/r$,
and the actual radius of the round sphere is $a=r/2$.

The positive volume measure compatible with the coframe orientation is
$r^3\sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta\,d\varphi\,d\psi/64$.
The sign of the angular coordinate ordering is distinguished from the
coframe orientation. The volumes and local invariants are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Vol}(S^3)&=\frac{\pi^2r^3}{4}=2\pi^2a^3,\\
\operatorname{Vol}(S^3\times S^1)&=\frac{\pi^2Lr^3}{4},\\
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{LC}}&=\frac{24}{r^2}=\frac6{a^2},\\
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{EC}}&=\frac{24-6\eta^2}{r^2}-\frac23V^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M3-02}\label{eq:M3-02}
\end{equation}
It follows that
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{S^3}_{\mathrm{FULL}}(r)
 &=\frac{\pi^2L}{12\kappa^2}
   \left[V^2r^3+(9\eta^2-36)r\right],\\
U^{S^3}_{\mathrm{TT}}(r)=U^{S^3}_{\mathrm{REE}}(r)
 &=\frac{\pi^2L}{12\kappa^2}
   \left[V^2r^3+12V\eta\kappa^2\theta r^2+(9\eta^2-36)r\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{M3-03}\label{eq:M3-03}
\end{equation}
To express the axial component as $2\chi/a$ in terms of the actual radius,
we also transform $\chi=\eta/2$. The same fixed-$\chi$ path gives
\begin{equation}
U^{S^3}_{\mathrm{FULL}}(a)
=\frac{2\pi^2L}{3\kappa^2}
\left[V^2a^3+9(\chi^2-1)a\right].
\tag{M3-04}\label{eq:M3-04}
\end{equation}
For each diagnostic action, add $12V\chi\kappa^2\theta a^2$ inside the
brackets. Replacing the radius symbol alone while keeping the definition
of the axial coefficient unchanged is not this transformation.

\subsection{The three-torus: periods and expansion paths}
Let $x^i\in[0,2\pi)$ and $e^{i-1}=R_i\,dx^i$.
The positive coframe coefficients $R_i$ correspond to actual circumferences
$\ell_i=2\pi R_i$. Structure coefficients and Levi--Civita curvature vanish,
but the EC curvature with torsion need not vanish:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Vol}(T^3\times S^1)=(2\pi)^3LR_1R_2R_3,\qquad
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{EC}}=-\frac{6\eta^2}{R_1^2}-\frac23V^2
\tag{M3-05}\label{eq:M3-05}
\end{equation}
Along the isotropic path $R_i=r$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{T^3}_{\mathrm{FULL}}(r)
 &=\frac{8\pi^3L}{3\kappa^2}\left[V^2r^3+9\eta^2r\right],\\
U^{T^3}_{\mathrm{TT}}(r)=U^{T^3}_{\mathrm{REE}}(r)
 &=\frac{8\pi^3L}{3\kappa^2}
   \left[V^2r^3+12V\eta\kappa^2\theta r^2+9\eta^2r\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{M3-06}\label{eq:M3-06}
\end{equation}
For fixed positive shape ratios $R_2=\alpha R_1$, $R_3=\beta R_1$,
set $r=R_1$ and multiply the potential by $\alpha\beta$.
The radius classification is unchanged, but stability under shape
variations does not follow. Writing the axial component as
$2\zeta/\ell_1$ requires $\zeta=2\pi\eta$.

\subsection{Nil geometry: compact quotient and lattice}
Define the Heisenberg group $H$ by
$(x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+xy')$, with coframe
\begin{equation}
e^0=R\,dx,\qquad e^1=R\,dy,\qquad
e^2=R(dz-x\,dy),\qquad R>0.
\tag{M3-07}\label{eq:M3-07}
\end{equation}
The nonzero structure coefficients are $C^2{}_{01}=-1/R$ and
$C^2{}_{10}=1/R$. We use the left quotient $\Gamma\backslash H$,
where
$\Gamma=\{(2\pi j,2\pi k,4\pi^2l):j,k,l\in\mathbb Z\}$.
For general lattice periods $(a,b,c)$, closure under the group product
requires $ab/c\in\mathbb Z$. Our choice
$(a,b,c)=(2\pi,2\pi,4\pi^2)$ has $ab/c=1$.
The coframe descends to the quotient and the spatial manifold is compact.

A fundamental integration region is
$x,y\in[0,2\pi)$, $z\in[0,4\pi^2)$, with the twisted face identifications
\begin{equation}
(x,y,z)\sim(x+2\pi,y,z+2\pi y),\quad
(x,y,z)\sim(x,y+2\pi,z),\quad
(x,y,z)\sim(x,y,z+4\pi^2).
\tag{M3-08}\label{eq:M3-08}
\end{equation}
This is not a product torus with three independently periodic coordinates.
The volume form is $R^3dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$.
The two base circumferences are $2\pi R$, and the central fiber circumference
is $4\pi^2R$. We hold this lattice fixed while varying $R$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Vol}(\mathrm{Nil}^3\times S^1)&=(2\pi)^4LR^3,\\
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{LC}}&=-\frac1{2R^2},\\
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{EC}}&=-\frac{1/2+6\eta^2}{R^2}-\frac23V^2
\end{aligned}
\tag{M3-09}\label{eq:M3-09}
\end{equation}
The reduced potentials are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{\mathrm{Nil}}_{\mathrm{FULL}}(R)
 &=\frac{4\pi^4L}{3\kappa^2}
   \left[4V^2R^3+(36\eta^2+3)R\right],\\
U^{\mathrm{Nil}}_{\mathrm{TT}}(R)=U^{\mathrm{Nil}}_{\mathrm{REE}}(R)
 &=\frac{4\pi^4L}{3\kappa^2}
   \left[4V^2R^3+48V\eta\kappa^2\theta R^2+(36\eta^2+3)R\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{M3-10}\label{eq:M3-10}
\end{equation}
Local structure coefficients alone do not determine the lattice.
The quotient above is part of our definition of the geometry, and provides
a concrete global realization of the volume $(2\pi)^4LR^3$.

\subsection{Common structure}
On all three geometries,
$T_{abc}T^{abc}=24\eta^2/s^2+2V^2/3$, and the NY densities obey
\eqref{eq:M2-07}. Denoting the common scale by $r$, each potential has
the form $U=P(Ar^3+Br^2+Cr)$ with $P>0$.
FULL has $B=0$, whereas the diagnostic actions have
$B\propto V\eta\theta$. The coefficient $C$ contains the contribution
of the Levi--Civita curvature.

A positive constant change of volume normalization multiplies $U$,
its derivatives, and its heights by the same factor at fixed input,
without changing positive-radius extremum locations or their classification.
Conversion to an actual radius or circumference also requires transforming
the horizontal coordinate and the axial parameter.
