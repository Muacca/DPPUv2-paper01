\section{Numerical Results: Phase Diagrams and Boundaries}
\label{sec:numerical}

This section presents the results of numerical scanning on the effective 
potentials derived in Sec.~\ref{sec:reductions}. We first explain the Type 
classification procedure and how to read phase diagrams, then show the phase 
diagrams for each topology. The mechanisms of phase boundary formation and 
geometric interpretation are discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanisms}.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Type Classification Procedure}
\label{sec:classification_procedure}

\subsubsection{Search region and numerical method}

For each parameter point $(V, \eta, \theta_{\NY})$, we numerically search for 
extrema of $\Veff(r)$ in the range $r \in [r_{\min}, r_{\max}] = [0.01, 10^6]$. 
We use Brent's method (\texttt{scipy.optimize.minimize\_scalar}) for extremum 
search and verify the curvature condition $\dd^2 V/\dd r^2 > 0$ by numerical 
differentiation.

\subsubsection{Type determination criteria}

Based on the definitions in Sec.~\ref{sec:type_definition}, we determine Types 
using the following criteria:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Type I (stable with barrier)}: A local minimum $r_0$ exists 
        within the allowed region, and a barrier ($\Delta V > 0$) exists in 
        the $r \to 0$ direction
  \item \textbf{Type II (rolling)}: A local minimum $r_0$ exists within the 
        allowed region, but no barrier in the $r \to 0$ direction 
        ($\dd\Veff/\dd r|_{r \to 0^+} < 0$)
  \item \textbf{Type III (unstable)}: No stable local minimum exists within 
        the allowed region, or search reaches boundary ($r_{\min}$ or $r_{\max}$)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Scan resolution}

The scan resolution is 51 points in the $V$ direction, 151 points in the 
$\eta$ direction, and 51 points in the $\theta_{\NY}$ direction (range $[0, 5]$), 
giving approximately 390,000 evaluations per topology. Note that this paper 
primarily presents results for $\theta_{\NY} \lesssim 2$. The uncertainty in 
boundary positions is on the order of the grid spacing 
($\Delta\eta \approx 0.1$, $\Delta V \approx 0.1$).

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{How to Read Phase Diagrams}
\label{sec:reading_diagrams}

In the phase diagrams of this paper, Type classification is displayed on the 
$(V, \eta)$ plane using the following visual conventions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Colored region (no hatching)}: Type I (metastable well with barrier)
  \item \textbf{Colored region (with hatching)}: Type II (rolling, no barrier)
  \item \textbf{White region}: Type III (unstable / boundary-attached)
\end{itemize}

The color gradient represents the logarithmic value of the stable radius 
$\log_{10}(r_0)$, ranging from purple (small) to yellow (large). White contour 
lines show the logarithmic barrier height/well depth $\log_{10}(\Delta V)$.

\subsection{Phase Diagrams by Topology: FULL Variant}
\label{sec:phase_diagrams}

\subsubsection{$\Sthree \times \Sone$}

Figure~\ref{fig:phase_S3} shows the phase diagrams for $\Sthree$-FULL at 
$\theta_{\NY} = 0, 1, 2$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig09_phase_diagram_S3-FULL.png}
  \caption{Phase diagram: $\Sthree \times \Sone$ ($\theta_{\NY} = 0, 1, 2$).}
  \label{fig:phase_S3}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{For $\theta_{\NY} = 0$:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $|\eta| \gtrsim 2$: Type III (white)
  \item $|\eta| \lesssim 2$: Band-like region of Type II (hatched). 
        This corresponds exactly to the region where $C = 9\eta^2 - 36 < 0$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{For $\theta_{\NY} > 0$:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Type I (stable well) appears in regions where $\eta$ is large.
  \item Type II (rolling) persists in the central band $|\eta| \lesssim 2$.
  \item A characteristic "transition" (Type I $\to$ Type II $\to$ Type III) occurs 
        as $\eta$ scans across the origin (e.g., $-3 \to 0 \to +3$).
        This reflects the sign change of $C(\eta)$.
  \item $\eta \to -\eta$ asymmetry is introduced by the $B$ term.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Key observations:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\theta_{\NY} = 0$: Stability (Type II) is purely geometric ($C<0$).
  \item $\theta_{\NY} > 0$: $B$ term enables barrier formation (Type I) at $\eta < 0$ area outside the central band.
  \item The phase boundary consistently aligns with $C=0$ ($\eta=\pm 2$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{$\Tthree \times \Sone$}

Figure~\ref{fig:phase_T3} shows the phase diagrams for $\Tthree$-FULL at 
$\theta_{\NY} = 0, 1, 2$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig10_phase_diagram_T3-FULL.png}
  \caption{Phase diagram: $\Tthree \times \Sone$ ($\theta_{\NY} = 0, 1, 2$).}
  \label{fig:phase_T3}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{For $\theta_{\NY} = 0$:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Entire region is Type III (monotonically increasing)
  \item No stable minimum exists
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{For $\theta_{\NY} > 0$:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Type I (stable well) appears in the $\eta < 0$ region where $B < 0$
  \item $\eta \gtrsim 0$ is entirely Type III
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Key observations:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\theta_{\NY} = 0$: No stability (Geometric instability).
  \item $\theta_{\NY} > 0$: Torsion-induced stability. Type I appears.
  \item $\eta \to -\eta$ symmetry is broken
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{$\Nilthree \times \Sone$}

Figure~\ref{fig:phase_Nil3} shows the phase diagrams for $\Nilthree$-FULL at 
$\theta_{\NY} = 0, 1, 2$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig11_phase_diagram_Nil3-FULL.png}
  \caption{Phase diagram: $\Nilthree \times \Sone$ ($\theta_{\NY} = 0, 1, 2$).}
  \label{fig:phase_Nil3}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{For $\theta_{\NY} = 0$:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Entire region is Type III. Since $C = 36\eta^2 + 3 > 0$ always, and $B=0$,
        no minimum can form geometrically.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{For $\theta_{\NY} > 0$:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Type I (stable well) appears in the $\eta < 0$ region where $B < 0$.
  \item $\eta \gtrsim 0$ is entirely Type III
  \item Type III leakage Near $\eta \approx 0$, even on the negative side,
        a Type III region persists. This is because $B^2 \propto \eta^2$ vanishes
        faster than $C \to 3$ as $\eta \to 0$, failing the stability condition.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Key observations:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\theta_{\NY} = 0$: No stability (Geometric instability).
  \item $\theta_{\NY} > 0$: Torsion-induced stability, but inhibited by negative curvature near $\eta = 0$.
  \item $\eta \to -\eta$ symmetry is broken.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with TT/REE Variants}
\label{sec:variant_comparison}

This subsection compares the phase structure across three topologies
($\Sthree$, $\Tthree$, $\Nilthree$) and three Nieh--Yan variants (FULL, TT, REE)
as the coupling constant $\theta_{\NY}$ increases.

\paragraph{Earlier stabilization of the TT variant (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase_matrix_04to05}):}

\begin{itemize}
  \item At $\theta_{\NY} = 0.5$, the TT variant develops a stable region (Type~I)
        that is absent in the FULL and REE variants at the same coupling.
  \item For $\Sthree$, stable regions already exist in all variants at
        $\theta_{\NY} < 0.5$; however, at $\theta_{\NY} = 0.5$ the Type~I region
        in the TT variant expands significantly toward lower $\eta$.
        This behavior is consistent with curvature-assisted stabilization in
        $\Sthree$, where positive background curvature cooperates with torsional
        contributions.
  \item For $\Tthree$ and $\Nilthree$, which remain entirely Type~III (unstable)
        at $\theta_{\NY} < 0.5$, the TT variant is the first to develop a Type~I
        region in the $\eta < 0$ domain upon reaching $\theta_{\NY} = 0.5$.
  \item The $r^2$ coefficient $B$ in the TT variant is larger in magnitude than
        in the FULL and REE variants ($B_{\TT} = 2 B_{\FULL}$), providing a
        stronger stabilizing (negative) contribution to the effective potential.
        This likely explains why the TT sector satisfies the
        stability condition at smaller $\theta_{\NY}$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/fig12_phase_matrix_04to05.png}
  \caption{Phase diagram matrix: $\theta_{\NY} = 0.4$ to $0.5$.}
  \label{fig:phase_matrix_04to05}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Stabilization of FULL and REE variants (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase_matrix_08to09}):}

\begin{itemize}
  \item For $\theta_{\NY} < 0.9$, the FULL and REE variants of $\Tthree$ and
        $\Nilthree$ remain Type~III. At $\theta_{\NY} = 0.9$, Type~I regions
        appear simultaneously in both variants, indicating that they have reached
        the same stabilization threshold previously crossed by the TT variant.
  \item In $\Nilthree$, the TT variant's stable region extends close to
        $\eta = 0$, whereas the FULL and REE variants retain a residual Type~III
        ``leakage'' near $\eta \approx 0$. This residual instability suggests a
        negative curvature-related suppression of stabilization specific to
        the Nil geometry.
  \item Across all three topologies, the phase diagrams of the FULL and REE
        variants are identical in shape. This indicates that the FULL and REE
        reductions converge to a common effective structure regardless of topology.
\end{itemize}
