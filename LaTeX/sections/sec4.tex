\section{Analytic classification on the positive-radius domain}\label{sec4}
\subsection{Definitions and cubic criterion}
The domain is $r>0$.
Type~I has a positive-radius local maximum followed by a strict local
minimum. Type~II has a strict local minimum without a preceding
positive-radius maximum. Type~III has no strict local minimum;
it includes monotone, runaway, flat, and stationary-inflection cases.
A numerical failure is not a physical Type~III result.

For $U=P(Ar^3+Br^2+Cr)$ with $P,A>0$, define
\begin{equation}
U'=P(3Ar^2+2Br+C),\qquad
D=B^2-3AC,\qquad
r_\pm=\frac{-B\pm\sqrt D}{3A}.
\tag{M4-01}\label{eq:M4-01}
\end{equation}
For $D>0$, the second derivatives at the two roots are
$U''(r_\pm)=\pm2P\sqrt D$, so a positive $r_+$ is the strict-minimum
candidate. The signs of the roots give necessary and sufficient conditions.
The complete criterion is:
\begin{itemize}
\item $C<0$: Type~II. The product of the roots, $C/(3A)$, is negative,
so only $r_+$ is positive; no additional $B<0$ condition is needed.
\item $C=0$ and $B<0$: Type~II, with $r_+=-2B/(3A)>0$;
the root at $r=0$ is excluded.
\item $C>0$, $B<0$, and $D>0$: Type~I, with $0<r_-<r_+$.
\item Otherwise: Type~III. In particular, $D=0$ does not give a strict minimum.
\end{itemize}
This criterion does not depend on a finite search window.
The condition $C>0$ alone is insufficient for a barrier and minimum:
the sign of $B$ and the discriminant are essential.

\subsection{Complete action}
For $S^3$, $A=V^2$ and $C=9\eta^2-36$.
When $V\ne0$,
\begin{equation}
\lvert\eta\rvert<2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{type-II},\qquad
r_0=\frac{\sqrt{12-3\eta^2}}{\lvert V\rvert}.
\tag{M4-02}\label{eq:M4-02}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $a_0=r_0/2$ and $|\chi|<1$.
The condition is independent of $\theta$ and symmetric under $\eta\mapsto-\eta$.

For the isotropic or fixed-shape torus, $C=9\eta^2\ge0$;
the FULL potential is increasing, except that it is flat when both
torsion parameters vanish. For Nil, $C=36\eta^2+3>0$ and FULL is
strictly increasing. There is no Type~I region in FULL on these paths
and no stabilization driven by the complete NY term.

\subsection{Diagnostic actions}
For $S^3$ with $V\ne0$, $|\eta|<2$ gives Type~II for any mixed coefficient.
At $|\eta|=2$, Type~II requires $V\eta\theta<0$.
For $|\eta|>2$, Type~I occurs precisely when
\begin{equation}
V\eta\theta<0,\qquad
16\eta^2\kappa^4\theta^2>3(\eta^2-4).
\tag{M4-03}\label{eq:M4-03}
\end{equation}
For the isotropic or fixed-shape torus, Type~I requires
\begin{equation}
V\eta\theta<0,\qquad
\kappa^2\lvert\theta\rvert>\frac{\sqrt3}{4}.
\tag{M4-04}\label{eq:M4-04}
\end{equation}
For Nil, the condition is
\begin{equation}
V\eta\theta<0,\qquad
(64\kappa^4\theta^2-12)\eta^2>1.
\tag{M4-05}\label{eq:M4-05}
\end{equation}
Equality at the discriminant boundary gives a stationary inflection,
not a strict minimum. These are conditions for TT and positive REE,
not for FULL.

\subsection{Degenerate parameters and numerical boundaries}
At $V=0$, this ansatz has $A=B=0$, so the potential is linear.
On $S^3$, it runs away for $|\eta|<2$, is flat at $|\eta|=2$, and
increases for $|\eta|>2$.
On $T^3$, it is flat at $\eta=0$ and otherwise increases.
On Nil, it always increases.
The generic quadratic case $A=0$, $B>0$, $C<0$ would give Type~II,
but it is not the $V=0$ specialization of this ansatz.

In floating-point evaluation, if
$D\le64\epsilon_{\mathrm{float}}\max(B^2,|3AC|)$,
the implementation reports no resolved strict minimum.
Exact $D=0$ and the numerically unresolved neighborhood of that boundary
are distinct: in particular, a small positive discriminant is not proved
to be zero by this test. Analytic conditions take precedence.
Appendix~\ref{appC} distinguishes analytic classification from
search-window and numerical-error behavior.
