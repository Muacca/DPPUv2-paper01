\section{Extremum values and their interpretation}\label{sec5}
\subsection{Local curvature and potential height}
At a strict cubic minimum $r_0=r_+$,
\begin{equation}
U(r_0)=-P r_0^2(2Ar_0+B),\qquad
U''(r_0)=2P\sqrt{B^2-3AC}.
\tag{M5-01}\label{eq:M5-01}
\end{equation}
The second derivative is the curvature of the one-variable reduced
potential. It is neither a mass squared nor a statement that the full
Hessian is positive.

For Type~I, the height difference between the local maximum and minimum is
\begin{equation}
\Delta U_{\mathrm I}
=U(r_-)-U(r_+)
=\frac{4P(B^2-3AC)^{3/2}}{27A^2}>0.
\tag{M5-02}\label{eq:M5-02}
\end{equation}
For Type~II, we instead use the depth relative to the algebraic value
$U(0)=0$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta U_{\mathrm{II}}
=-U(r_+)
=P r_+^2(2Ar_++B)>0.
\tag{M5-03}\label{eq:M5-03}
\end{equation}
The point $r=0$ is a degenerate geometry outside the domain.
This depth is not a barrier between two positive-radius extrema.

\subsection{The FULL minimum on the sphere}
For $V\ne0$ and $|\eta|<2$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_0&=\frac{\sqrt{12-3\eta^2}}{\lvert V\rvert},\\
U(r_0)&=-\frac{\pi^2L}{6\kappa^2}
        \frac{(12-3\eta^2)^{3/2}}{\lvert V\rvert},\\
U''(r_0)&=\frac{\pi^2L}{2\kappa^2}
          \lvert V\rvert\sqrt{12-3\eta^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{M5-04}\label{eq:M5-04}
\end{equation}
The negative linear term reflects the competition between positive
spatial curvature and axial torsion, while the positive cubic term
comes from vector torsion. The NY coupling does not enter this mechanism.
As $V\to0$, the radius tends to infinity and the finite minimum is lost
to the runaway limit. As $|\eta|\to2$, the radius approaches the excluded
origin. With zero torsion,
$U=-3\pi^2Lr/\kappa^2$, so there is no finite well.
Curvature alone does not create this minimum.

\subsection{Distinct boundaries}
In the region $C>0$, $B<0$, the condition that the diagnostic minimum
have the same height as the algebraic origin is
\begin{equation}
B^2=4AC,\qquad r_+=-\frac{B}{2A}.
\tag{M5-05}\label{eq:M5-05}
\end{equation}
This is distinct from the saddle-node condition $B^2=3AC$.
At the saddle-node the strict minimum is lost, whereas at $B^2=4AC$
it remains and crosses the reference height.
The Type~I/II distinction is determined by the presence of a
positive-radius maximum, not by the minimum's height relative to the origin.

\subsection{Signs, normalization, and comparisons}
FULL depends on $V^2$ and $\eta^2$.
The diagnostic mixed term depends on $V\eta\theta$ and is invariant under
simultaneous reversal of $V$ and $\eta$, as in the $T/\tau$ dictionary.
Reversing only $\eta$ generally changes the diagnostic potential.

A positive prefactor preserves radii and phase types but scales all
potential heights. Comparing different geometries requires specifying
the global quotient, units, and physical quantities held fixed.
